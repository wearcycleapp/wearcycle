Privacy at Wearcycle

Last updated October 7, 2026. Wearcycle is a personal wardrobe app. This notice explains what it collects, why, where it is stored, who processes it, and what you can do about it. It follows the ten principles of Canada's private-sector privacy law (PIPEDA).

Who is responsible

The Wearcycle owner is accountable for your information. Questions, requests and complaints: wearcycle.app@gmail.com. You will get an answer within 30 days.

What is collected and why

InformationWhy
Email address and password (stored hashed, never readable)To sign you in and send sign-in emails (confirmation, password reset).
Photos of your clothes, cut-outs, and the details you or Claude fill in (name, category, colours, condition, notes, price paid if you add it)To show your closet and build outfits from it.
Outfit log (what you wore and when) and your settings (style, shopping department, dress code, reminders, holidays, language)To rotate outfits, spot unworn pieces and remind you about care and laundry.

Wearcycle does not collect anything else: no ads, no tracking or analytics, no selling or sharing for marketing.

Who processes it, and where

  • Supabase, Inc. (a US company) hosts your account, database, photos and the small server function that talks to Claude. Row-level security means only your signed-in account can read your rows and files. Supabase also keeps sign-in and server logs, which include your IP address.
  • Anthropic (Claude), United States. When Claude reads a photo you add, checks a piece's condition, learns a look from a photo, or suggests what to buy, that photo or a text list of your clothes is sent to Anthropic's API. Anthropic states that it deletes API inputs and outputs within 30 days (longer only if a request is flagged for breaking its usage policy) and does not train its models on them. You can turn Claude features off in Settings.
  • Open-Meteo (forecast) receives rounded coordinates, and Zippopotam.us receives a postal code you type. Only if you turn on weather.
  • Google (Gmail) delivers sign-in emails to your address.
  • GitHub Pages serves the app's files; like any web host it receives your IP address and browser details when you open the app.
  • Google Maps and Google Search open only when you tap a link: to find a donation drop-off, tailor or repair shop, or to shop for a piece Wearcycle suggests. Google then receives that search (for example "navy chinos"), not your closet.
  • Done on your phone, sent nowhere: background removal for cut-outs, outfit scoring, and fonts (served from Wearcycle itself).

Outside Canada. Your information is processed by these providers in the United States and possibly other countries. While it is there, it can be accessed by the courts, law enforcement and national security authorities of those countries under their laws. Each provider is bound by its own terms and privacy commitments to protect it.

Photos of other people

When you teach Wearcycle a look from a photo, use a photo of yourself or one you may use. That photo is sent to Claude to name the pieces and is not saved; only the description is kept.

How long it is kept

Your information is kept while you have an account. Delete anything at any time; deleting your account removes your clothes, photos, outfit log, settings and sign-in. The copy kept on your phone for offline use is removed when you sign out or delete your data. Copies in providers' backups and logs are removed on their own schedules.

Your rights and choices

  • Access and copy: Settings › Privacy and your data › Download my data gives you everything in one .zip (a JSON file plus your photos).
  • Correct: edit any piece, outfit or setting in the app, or ask us.
  • Withdraw consent: turn off Claude features or weather at any time; delete your data and account in Settings. Without an account the app cannot store a closet for you.
  • Complain: write to wearcycle.app@gmail.com first. If you are not satisfied, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

Safeguards and breaches

Connections use HTTPS, photos sit in a private storage bucket, each account can read only its own rows, passwords are hashed by Supabase Auth, and server keys never reach the app. If a breach creates a real risk of significant harm, we will tell you and the Privacy Commissioner as soon as feasible, and every breach is recorded and the record kept for 24 months, as Canadian law requires.

Children

Wearcycle is not meant for children under 13, and they should not create an account.

Changes

If this notice changes in a way that matters, the app shows a short summary and asks again before carrying on.
