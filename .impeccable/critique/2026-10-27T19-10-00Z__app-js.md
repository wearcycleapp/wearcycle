## Priority issues

- **P1 No answer first.** The screen opens with the season checklist, not with what to buy. The person must read 15 blocks to find the two or three purchases that matter. Principle 3 ("buying is the last step and only for a real gap") is honoured in the data, but the layout gives every list equal weight.
- **P1 Owned items fill the screen.** Build toward a style shows every owned essential as a full row (16 of 17); the season checklist shows met items as full rows (5 of 8); covered occasions take a panel each. Most of the 5.6 screens say "you have this".
- **P1 Same purchase, three places.** Gaps per occasion, More outfits from one purchase and Build toward a style each produce suggestions with their own ranking and wording, so the person compares lists instead of choosing.
- **P2 Clothes are not the hero.** Apart from the season preview, purchases show a 12px swatch and text; the owned pieces each purchase pairs with are named, not shown.
- **P2 Wide screens are a stretched phone list** (one column at 1366, 3.3 screens).

## Strengths

- Every suggestion is tied to what the person owns ("+16 outfits", "pairs with ...") and covered occasions say so plainly, as PRODUCT.md asks.
- Copy is short and honest; no invented need.

## Persona red flags

- A new user with a small closet sees six occasion panels of gaps at once.
- A French reader at 360px scrolls 6.3 screens.
