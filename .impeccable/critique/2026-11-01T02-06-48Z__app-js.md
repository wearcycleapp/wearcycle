## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: The cream canvas behind cut-outs and the garment glyphs are specific to Wearcycle, but only some tiles get the canvas (glyph tiles sit on the white surface), and the cards, shadows and three text lines are a generic catalog card that any inventory app could use. The garment detail is split: the preview is product-editorial (picture first, full width), the editor is a form with a 110px thumbnail beside two buttons.

**Deterministic scan**: the detector found nothing on the rendered Closet, editor and preview (0 warnings, 0 advisories). axe-core found the chip-count contrast on every Closet view. The detector does not judge image sharpness or byte cost; the measurements above do.

## Overall Impression

The Closet works, but it is still the v1.x catalog: records first, garments second, soft pictures on phones. The biggest opportunity is the GALLERY promise: let the picture fill a flat square (phones) or 4:5 (iPad, Windows) tile, say only name, condition and wears, and choose each picture by its drawn size.

## What's Working

- Cut-outs on the cream canvas already look like a wardrobe, and the drop shadow makes them sit on it.
- The piece preview is picture first with a clear previous and next pager (v1.51.0) and a zoom affordance.
- The editor keeps every field one tap away under More details, with a short summary line in the closed state.

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] Pictures soft on phones, bytes spent where they are not seen**: phone tiles draw the 256px thumbnail at up to 2.0x, while every cut-out in the closet is downloaded at app open and the editor downloads the full photo for a 110px box. **Fix**: choose each picture by drawn size x pixel density (cut-out wherever it exists; thumbnail while sharp; the 1024px copy, else the full photo), load Closet pictures only near the screen. **Suggested command**: /impeccable optimize
- **[P1] Garment not the hero in the item editor**: 110x110 picture beside the photo buttons. **Fix**: picture first and full width on phones; on wide screens a centred sheet with the picture on about 60% and the fields on 40%. **Suggested command**: /impeccable layout
- **[P1] Categories hidden and counts unreadable**: on phones 3 of 10 category chips show in full and the row hides its scrollbar; counts are 3.41:1. **Fix**: chips that wrap (A) and text tabs (B), counts at 12px without opacity (5.88:1). **Suggested command**: /impeccable adapt
- **[P2] Tiles read as records**: condition dot, pills and the occasions line; no wear count; attention as a color-only dot. **Fix**: name, then "condition · N wears"; flags as short words on the picture (In the wash, Needs repair, Review, Example, Needs attention). **Suggested command**: /impeccable distill
- **[P2] Leftovers**: 17 overridden declarations, #f3f0ea in the viewer, the duplicated accessible name. **Fix**: remove with an exact pixel compare; canvas tokens; empty alt on tile pictures. **Suggested command**: /impeccable polish

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)**: at 390x844 the first screen shows two full tiles; looking for shoes means swiping a chip row whose scrollbar is hidden; pictures are soft at 2.0x.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent)**: Needs attention is a dot with a title, so it is invisible to a screen reader on touch and to anyone who cannot tell red from green; tiles with a picture announce their name twice; chip counts fail 4.5:1.

**Jordan (First-Timer)**: "Retire" with a red dot on a tile reads like an instruction; the occasions line ("No occasion fits") on a tile raises a question the tile cannot answer.

## Minor Observations

- The In the wash view replaces the grid with a panel; that is fine, but the chip that opens it sits among the categories.
- The zoom viewer draws cut-outs on #f3f0ea, not the cream canvas tokens.
