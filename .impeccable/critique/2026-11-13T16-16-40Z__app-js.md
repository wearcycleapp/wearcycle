## Design Specificity Verdict

The page is a sign-up form with a brand row. It is specific in its words (weather, occasion, repair, donate) but not in what it shows: a new visitor sees a flat lay of glyphs and a form, never the thing only Wearcycle does, a ranked outfit for today's weather with the reasons behind it.

## Overall Impression

Phase 13 made the page correct and accessible; it did not make it persuasive. For the public launch (claude/launch-plan.md, Phase A free beta) the first screen has to sell the morning moment before asking for an account.

## What's Working

- Brand row, headline "Get dressed in seconds." and the Ink & Coral world are distinctive.
- The language button is at the top (seven languages).
- Honest consent that names who receives the data.

## Priority Issues

- [P1] No proof of the product. Measured: 2 images (logo and decoration) and 18 SVGs, of which none is an outfit card, a match label, a reason or the weather. The three perks that describe the product start at 962px (390x844) and 980px (360x800), below the first screen.
- [P1] Commitment before value. Create account sits at 847 to 895px on a 360x800 phone (below the first screen) after 3 fields and a 35-word consent; no way to look at an example first.
- [P1] The consent says "I am 13 or older"; the owner decided 18+ for public sign-ups (launch plan L2, step A7 not built yet).
- [P2] Text: tagline 9px (phones) and 10px; promise 31 words; the version line inside the sign-up card (internal, not for a visitor); "Flat-lay view of every look" is jargon.
- [P2] Wide windows: at 1366x768 the page is a 2-column split with a 384px picture; at 1920x1080 everything sits in 1200px.
- [P2] Nothing about what is free, what it costs in time (photos), the privacy stance (no ads, no tracking, download and delete) or the platforms, all true facts the visitor would weigh.

## Persona Red Flags

- Jordan (first-timer, phone, from a link): reads a headline and a form; has to imagine what an outfit pick looks like; leaves before typing an email.
- Sam (Windows, 1920x1080): a small picture and a card in the middle of a large window.
- A parent setting up the family: does not learn that it works on Android, iPad and Windows, or in seven languages, until after signing up.

## Minor Observations

- The sign-in tab and the Forgot password link are fine for returning users; keep them one tap away.
- The perks use ink ticks (good, Phase 13); they could become the shown examples.

## Questions to Consider

- If the page had no words, would a visitor know it picks an outfit from their own clothes?
- Should a visitor be able to try the example closet before giving an email?
